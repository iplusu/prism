 \textbf{Novelty scope.} LoRA SFT, focal loss, and calibrated abstention (Chow-style reject options) are each standard; the contribution is their coupling in a measured loop on execution-oracle-validated LLM-generated code, with a falsifiable hypothesis as the primary artifact, plus a bounded-evidence labeling protocol, a cost-quality Pareto against LLM judges, and an escalation benchmark under budget as secondary artifacts. We claim no new SFT algorithm, detector architecture, or first-of-kind corpus. Related work targets different mechanisms --- SIVA [7] (prompt/memory adaptation), IRIS [8] (hybrid static analysis), SSVD [9] (pseudo-label learning), SamplingVD [20] (imbalance sampling), SPLVD [21] (self-paced sample selection), Sifting the Noise [10] (agentic FP triage), CodeSecEval [2] (execution-guided generator refinement), MetaMorphSES [22] (generator-detector co-evolution) --- and we do not claim an absence of execution oracles or matched-budget controls in that work. Our test concerns FP/FN-targeted scheduling for a post-hoc verifier on a frozen spec-driven generation distribution, with explicit label-acquisition and adaptation budgets, compared against non-targeted schedules rather than a claim that execution feedback or adaptive detection is new.
